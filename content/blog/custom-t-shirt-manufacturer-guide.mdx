Whether you're launching a streetwear brand, running a Shopify store, or scaling Amazon FBA private-label products, finding the right custom t-shirt manufacturer is one of the most important business decisions you'll make. The wrong partner means missed deadlines, poor quality, and wasted capital. The right one becomes a competitive advantage.

This guide covers everything you need to know — how to find manufacturers, what to ask, how to vet them, and what the process looks like from first inquiry to delivery.

## Why T-Shirt Manufacturing Matters More Than You Think

A t-shirt seems simple. But behind every great t-shirt is a series of critical decisions: fabric weight and composition, cut and construction, printing method, wash treatment, and finishing. Each choice directly affects how the garment looks, feels, performs, and holds up over time.

A manufacturer who understands these details — and communicates them clearly — is worth more than a dirt-cheap factory that doesn't.

## Types of Custom T-Shirt Manufacturing

Before you search, understand what type of manufacturing you need:

**Blank wholesale with logo printing** — You buy ready-made blank t-shirts and add your logo. MOQ is low (often 12–24 pieces per design), but your garment is identical to every other brand using the same blank.

**Custom cut & sew manufacturing** — You design a unique silhouette, specify the fabric, and the factory builds it from scratch. Higher MOQ (typically 50–100 pieces per style/colour), but the garment is truly yours. This is OEM manufacturing.

**Private label** — Similar to cut & sew but you use a manufacturer's existing designs with your own branding applied. Good middle ground.

For anyone building a brand with long-term ambitions, cut & sew OEM manufacturing is the right path. It's what we specialise in at [Potato Apparel](/about).

## Where to Find Custom T-Shirt Manufacturers

### 1. Alibaba and Made-in-China

The most accessible starting point for China-based manufacturers. Filter by "verified supplier" and "trade assurance" status. Look for factories with at least 3 years on the platform and strong review histories.

**Limitation:** You're often communicating with trading companies, not factories. Always ask for a factory audit report.

### 2. Industry Trade Shows

**MAGIC (Las Vegas)** and **Texworld (New York/Paris)** are the two biggest apparel sourcing shows. You meet factory representatives in person and can evaluate samples on the spot.

### 3. Referrals from other brand founders

Ask in apparel brand communities (Reddit's r/streetwear, private Facebook groups, Discord servers). Real founder recommendations are gold.

### 4. Direct outreach to manufacturers you admire

If you love the quality of a garment from another brand, check if it has any manufacturer information on the label or packaging. Sometimes you can trace it back.

## What to Look for in a Custom T-Shirt Manufacturer

### MOQ (Minimum Order Quantity)

The industry range for custom cut & sew t-shirts is 50–300 pieces per style/colour. Anything under 50 is exceptional; anything over 200 is standard mass-market territory.

**At Potato Apparel, our MOQ for custom t-shirts starts at 50 pieces per style per colour** — allowing startups and emerging brands to test designs without overcommitting.

### Sample turnaround time

A credible manufacturer can produce a pre-production sample in 7–15 business days. If they quote longer or are vague, that's a warning sign.

### Printing capabilities

Confirm whether decoration is done in-house or outsourced. In-house means better quality control. Key methods for t-shirts:

- **Screen printing** — Best for bold, simple designs at volume. Most cost-effective over 50+ units.
- **DTG (Direct-to-Garment)** — Best for photo-quality designs, gradients, small runs.
- **Embroidery** — Best for structured logos, badges, and hats. Adds premium feel.
- **Discharge printing** — Creates a soft-hand, vintage look. The ink removes the fabric dye rather than sitting on top.
- **Heat transfer** — Versatile for small quantities and special finishes.

### Fabric options

A capable manufacturer should be able to source:
- **100% ring-spun cotton** (180–220 gsm for standard, 250–280 gsm for premium/heavyweight)
- **Tri-blend** (polyester/cotton/rayon) for soft, drapey feel
- **Bamboo/organic cotton** for sustainable collections
- Custom fabric weights and yarns per your specification

### Quality control process

Ask specifically: how many QC stages do you have? A good answer includes inline QC (during production), semi-final QC (at ~80% completion), and final QC (before packing). Ask if they provide QC reports.

## Red Flags to Avoid

- **No physical samples before bulk** — Never approve production without reviewing a physical prototype.
- **Vague pricing** — Legitimate manufacturers can give you a price range within 48 hours of receiving your specs.
- **No factory photos or videos** — Every real manufacturer should be able to show you their facility.
- **Pressure to pay 100% upfront** — Standard terms are 30% deposit, 70% before shipment.
- **Too-good-to-be-true pricing** — If the quote is dramatically lower than competitors, investigate why before committing.
- **No QC documentation** — If they've never provided a client with a QC report, their quality process is informal at best.

## Choosing the Right T-Shirt Construction

**Standard tee construction** includes crew neck with ribbed collar, side seam, single-needle hem. This works for most brands.

**Premium construction upgrades:**
- Tear-away label (allows your branded neck label without the old label underneath)
- Woven neck label replacement (most professional option)
- Double-needle stitching throughout
- Set-in sleeve vs. drop shoulder (affects silhouette significantly)
- Elongated body length for streetwear aesthetic
- Custom ribbing weight and texture
