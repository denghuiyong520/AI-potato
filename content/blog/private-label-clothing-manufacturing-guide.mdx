Private label clothing is one of the fastest-growing segments in the fashion industry. Rather than reselling other brands' products, private label lets you create garments under your own brand name — at manufacturing costs — and sell them at full retail prices.

This guide covers everything: what private label means for clothing, how to find the right manufacturer, how to brand your garments, and how to scale from a first collection to a sustainable business.

## What Is Private Label Clothing?

Private label clothing means garments manufactured to your specification (or chosen from a manufacturer's existing designs), branded exclusively with your brand identity. Your name on the label. Your design. Your IP.

There are two main approaches:

**ODM (Original Design Manufacturer)** — You select from the manufacturer's existing styles and fabrics, add your branding, and sell under your name. Lower investment, faster to market.

**OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer)** — You provide or co-design the garment specifications. The manufacturer builds to your exact spec. Higher investment, fully unique product.

Most brands start with ODM to validate the market, then move to OEM as they grow. Both are valid — and both are true private label because the product carries your brand exclusively.

## Why Private Label Is the Right Model for Clothing Brands

Compared to dropshipping or reselling branded goods:

**Higher margins** — Manufacturing cost is $8–$20 for items retailing at $40–$120.

**Brand ownership** — You're building brand equity, not someone else's.

**Customer loyalty** — Your product is unique. Customers can only buy it from you.

**Pricing power** — No competitor can undercut you on the same SKU.

**Scalable** — The margin structure allows profitable marketing spend.

## Finding the Right Private Label Clothing Manufacturer

### What to look for

**Low MOQ for startups** — Look for manufacturers offering 50–100 pieces per style. Higher MOQs lock up capital before you know what sells.

**Full branding services** — Your private label partner should handle woven neck labels, size labels, hang tags, care labels, and packaging. All under your brand.

**NDA willingness** — Your designs should be protected. A professional manufacturer signs NDAs and does not share your designs with competitors.

**Quality documentation** — AQL inspection reports, fabric certificates, and pre-production samples are non-negotiable.

**Communication** — You need a dedicated contact who responds within 24 hours and understands garment construction.

### Where to find private label manufacturers

- **Alibaba** — Large supplier marketplace. Filter by "Gold Supplier" and review audit reports carefully.
- **Trade shows** — MAGIC (Las Vegas), Texworld (NYC), Première Vision (Paris)
- **Agency referrals** — Fashion sourcing agencies can match you to vetted factories
- **Direct cold outreach** — If you know which factories produce garments you admire, reach out directly

### Questions to ask potential manufacturers

1. What is your MOQ for private label orders?
2. Do you provide in-house branding (labels, hang tags, packaging)?
3. What's your sample lead time?
4. Can you show me private label work you've done for existing clients?
5. Do you sign NDAs?
6. What is your QC process and do you provide inspection reports?
7. What are your payment terms?

## Creating Your First Private Label Collection

### Start with a focused capsule

Don't try to launch with 20 SKUs. Start with 3–5 pieces that are:
- Complementary (they work as a collection)
- Your core customer's top-priority items
- Achievable at your budget (50–100 pieces each)

Typical first-collection structures:
- **Basics brand**: Heavyweight tee + hoodie + jogger
- **Streetwear brand**: Oversized tee + cargo pants + bomber jacket
- **Activewear brand**: Crop top + high-waist legging + sports bra
- **Elevated casual**: Premium tee + tailored shorts + light jacket

### The design and spec process

You don't need to be a trained designer. You need to be able to communicate:

1. **Reference garments** — Find existing products that capture the silhouette you want
2. **Fabric specifications** — Weight (GSM), content, feel preference
3. **Fit and sizing** — Tight/relaxed/oversized, body length, sleeve length
4. **Details** — Pockets, zippers, drawstrings, ribbing, hardware
5. **Decoration** — Where your logo goes, what technique (screen print, embroidery, patch)
6. **Branding** — Your label design, hang tag, size label, packaging

A [tech pack](/blog/how-to-create-a-tech-pack) formalises all of this into a document the factory uses as a production reference.

## Branding Your Private Label Garments

### Woven neck labels

The most professional option. Your brand name and logo woven in thread. Replaces the manufacturer's label completely. Usually requires 200+ units minimum for label production but the cost is low ($0.30–$0.60 per label).

### Printed inside neck label

A heat-applied or screen-printed label inside the collar. Lower cost than woven. Clean, premium look.

### Hang tags

Your brand's story, size guide, care instructions, and QR codes. Printed on your card stock to your design. Attach via string or safety pin.

### Care labels

Required by law in most markets. Should include fibre content, country of origin, and washing instructions in the appropriate languages for your market.

### Custom packaging

Polybags, tissue paper, branded boxes, ribbon, stickers. Your packaging is part of the brand experience — especially for DTC brands.

## MOQ and Budgeting

For private label clothing, your total first-collection investment depends on:

**Per-unit manufacturing cost** (ex-factory): $8–$30 depending on garment type  
**Branding costs** (labels, tags, packaging): $1–$5 per unit  
**Sample cost**: $30–$100 per prototype (usually credited on bulk)  
**Shipping**: $2–$8 per unit depending on method and destination  
**Import duties**: Varies by market (0–20% typically)

**Typical first collection budget ranges:**
- Bootstrap (50 pcs × 3 styles): $2,000–$5,000
- Early brand (100 pcs × 5 styles): $8,000–$20,000
- Funded launch (300 pcs × 8 styles): $30,000–$70,000

## Scaling Your Private Label Brand

Once your first collection validates what sells, the scaling path is clear:

1. **Increase quantities** on winning styles (lower per-unit cost, same product)
2. **Add colourways** (same pattern, different fabric or print)
3. **Expand the range** (add complementary SKUs)
4. **Move to OEM** if your ODM styles need adjustment (custom fabric, modified silhouette)
5. **Build in-season replenishment** (forecast based on sell-through data)

Your manufacturing relationship deepens over time — preferred pricing, faster turnarounds, priority production slots.

## Private Label Clothing for E-Commerce Sellers

If you're selling on Shopify, Amazon, or TikTok Shop, private label clothing offers significant advantages over reselling:

- **Amazon FBA**: Your unique ASIN means no one can hijack your listing or win the Buy Box away from you
- **Shopify DTC**: Full control of the brand story, pricing, and customer relationship
- **TikTok Shop**: Original products drive organic content creation by customers
