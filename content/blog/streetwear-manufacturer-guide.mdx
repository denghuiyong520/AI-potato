Streetwear is not just a category — it's a construction philosophy. Oversized silhouettes, heavyweight fabrics, bold graphics, premium finishes, and visible quality in every stitch. Finding a manufacturer who understands this — not just in theory but in production capability — is the critical challenge for every streetwear founder.

This guide is for brand founders who are serious about producing authentic streetwear and want to understand what separates capable manufacturers from the rest.

## What Makes Streetwear Manufacturing Different

Streetwear garments have specific requirements that most manufacturers designed for fast-fashion production can't easily meet:

**Heavy fabric weights** — True streetwear uses 280–420 gsm for tees and 350–500 gsm for hoodies. Most factories default to 160–180 gsm for t-shirts. A factory that primarily handles fast fashion may not have the machines or experience for heavyweight construction.

**Oversized pattern cutting** — Drop shoulders, extended body lengths, wide chest measurements. This requires specific pattern making expertise. Ask to see examples of oversized pieces they've produced.

**Specialty washes and treatments** — Acid wash, enzyme wash, stone wash, pigment wash. These are chemical processes done on finished garments. In-house capability is a major advantage.

**Complex graphic application** — High-definition screen printing with spot processes (discharge, puff, metallic), multi-position prints, foil application. Ask about their screen printing department specifically.

**Premium finishing** — Woven labels, custom hardware, branded zippers (YKK is the standard), heavyweight drawstrings, and stitch-out details that signal quality.

## Key Fabric Choices for Streetwear

### T-Shirts (280–420 gsm)

**Heavyweight ring-spun cotton** — The standard for premium streetwear tees. Single jersey or interlock construction. Ring-spun yarn creates a smoother surface and higher print quality than open-end spun.

**Garment-dyed construction** — Made in grey fabric then dyed after construction. Creates a vintage, slightly uneven tone that's highly sought after.

**Pigment-dyed** — Similar to garment dye but more dimensional. Slightly rough texture, faded aesthetic.

**Heavyweight French terry** — Popular for relaxed tee silhouettes in drop-shoulder cuts.

### Hoodies (350–500 gsm)

**Heavyweight brushed fleece** — 380–420 gsm is the sweet spot for premium streetwear. Brushed interior for softness without sacrificing structure.

**Loop-back fleece** — Unbrushed interior. More casual feel. Used in some contemporary brands for a "raw" aesthetic.

**Custom fleece with unique texture** — Waffle-knit panels, ribbed panels, or custom yarn blends can differentiate your collection.

### Bottoms

**Heavyweight fleece joggers** — Often the most reordered item in streetwear collections. Specify ankle construction (ribbed vs. zippered vs. drawstring).

**Cargo pants** — Complex construction with multiple pockets, hardware, and potentially technical fabrics. Requires pattern making expertise.

**Woven pieces** — Nylon windbreakers, track pants, and coach jackets require woven fabric expertise. Different machinery and skills than knitwear.

## Printing Techniques for Streetwear Graphics

### Screen Printing (Most Common)

Ink is pushed through a mesh screen onto the garment. Each colour is a separate screen. Best for bold, graphic designs at volume.

**Specialty screen printing processes popular in streetwear:**

**Discharge printing** — The ink removes the dye from the fabric rather than sitting on top. Creates a super-soft hand feel, slightly vintage look. The gold standard for premium tee graphics.

**Puff printing** — The ink expands when cured, creating a raised 3D texture. Very popular for logos and text.

**Metallic/foil printing** — Shiny metallic finishes applied over standard print. Adds luxury visual effect.

**Water-based inks** — More eco-friendly, softer hand feel than plastisol. Required for discharge printing.

**Plastisol inks** — The traditional standard. Slightly rubbery feel, very vivid and durable.

### Embroidery

For structured logos, badges, and text. Adds significant perceived value. Common positions: chest left logo, back neck, sleeve.

Multi-colour embroidery (6–12 threads) creates complex designs. Ask manufacturers about their digitising process — it determines final quality.

### All-Over Print (AOP)

Sublimation printing for 100% coverage designs. Requires polyester or poly-blend fabrics. Very popular for certain streetwear aesthetics.

**DTF (Direct to Film)** — A newer technique that can achieve all-over looks on cotton without heat transfer limitations.

## Construction Details That Signal Quality

Streetwear customers notice and care about:

**Stitch density** — More stitches per inch = stronger seam. Premium garments use 12–14 SPI on main seams.

**Bartacks at stress points** — Reinforcement stitching at pocket corners, hem splits, and strap attachment points.

**Cover stitch hems** — Two rows of topstitching on the outside, chain stitch underneath. Creates the clean, flat hem seen on premium garments.

**Custom ribbing** — Heavier-weight ribbing on cuffs and waistband than the standard. 200–250 gsm ribbing vs. 150 gsm standard.

**Hardware details** — YKK zippers (request by name), custom-branded metal zip pulls, cast metal aglets on drawstrings.

**Label quality** — Woven labels rather than printed, custom designed to brand specifications.

## Vetting a Streetwear Manufacturer

Before committing to sampling, ask:

**"Can you show me oversized/heavyweight garments you've produced?"** — Request photos and samples from actual production runs, not stock images.

**"What's your heaviest fleece GSM available?"** — If they max out at 300 gsm, they may not be equipped for true heavyweight streetwear.

**"Do you do in-house screen printing? Show me your discharge and puff print capability."** — Outsourced printing means less QC control.

**"What wash processes do you have in-house?"** — Garment washing and dyeing requires specialised equipment. Confirm it's on-site.

**"What's your MOQ for fully custom cut & sew pieces?"** — 50–100 pieces per style is the right range for an emerging brand.

**"Can you produce technical fabric pieces like coaches jackets?"** — If your brand extends to woven outerwear, this capability matters.

## Timelines and Costs for Streetwear Production

**Sampling:** 10–15 days for standard styles; 15–20 days for complex pieces

**Bulk production:** 25–35 days (slightly longer than basics due to more complex construction)

**Typical wholesale costs (50–100 units):**
- Heavyweight tee (300+ gsm, screen print): $10–$16
- Premium hoodie (380 gsm, screen print): $20–$28
- Cargo pants: $22–$35
- Coach jacket (woven): $28–$45

## Building a Long-Term Manufacturer Relationship

The best streetwear brands don't shop manufacturers — they invest in relationships. Benefits of a long-term manufacturing partnership:

- Preferred pricing on repeat orders
- Priority production scheduling during peak seasons
- Willingness to experiment with new techniques
- Access to new fabric developments
- Faster turnaround on reorders

Start small, prove you pay on time and communicate clearly, and you'll earn preferred customer status within 2–3 orders.

Great streetwear requires a manufacturer who can execute on construction quality, not just price. Heavyweight fabrics, specialty prints, and complex silhouettes are table stakes — not premium upgrades.
