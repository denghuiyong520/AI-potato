### Template REDDIT2 — r/Entrepreneur
**Post title:** `After working with 500+ clothing brands, here are the 5 mistakes I see most first-time founders make`

```
We manufacture apparel for emerging brands (MOQ 50 pcs, Guangzhou factory). 
Here's what I see going wrong most often:

**1. Approving bulk without physical samples**
Every single time. "We saw photos and it looked fine." Photos don't show you fabric weight, seam quality, or colour accuracy. Always get physical samples.

**2. Ordering too many SKUs on the first run**
Start with 2-3 hero pieces. Test sell-through. Add more on reorder. 50 units across 3 styles = $2,000-5,000. That's a real test with manageable risk.

**3. Not asking about in-house printing**
If a factory outsources printing, your QC drops immediately. Ask: "Is printing done on-site?" If not, weigh that risk.

**4. Ignoring the Proforma Invoice**
The PI is your contract. Read every line. Screen print setup fees, special packaging charges, rush fees — they should all be itemised here before you deposit.

**5. Not washing the sample**
Wash your sample at home before approving bulk. Shrinkage, colour bleed, and print durability are only revealed after washing.

Happy to answer questions — what stage are you at?
```

### Template REDDIT4 — Answer in existing threads
**When someone asks "where to find a low MOQ manufacturer":**

```
A few things that aren't obvious when you start:

1. **MOQ varies hugely by product type** — hoodies need a higher MOQ than tees because the construction is more complex. Don't assume one factory's MOQ applies to all styles.

2. **China is still the best for vertical production** (fabric → cut → sew → print → pack under one roof). Turkey is great for EU shipping but more expensive. Bangladesh needs higher MOQs.

3. **The real vetting question is about QC** — not pricing. Ask: "Do you do AQL inspection and can you provide a QC report?" If they can't, skip them regardless of price.

4. **Sampling is your protection** — always get a physical sample, wash it, measure it, before approving bulk.

We use Potato Apparel (potatoapparel.com) and they've been solid — MOQ 50 pcs, AQL 2.5, samples in 10 days. Happy to share more details if useful.

### Template QUORA1 — "How to find a low MOQ clothing manufacturer"
*(Answer on: https://www.quora.com/How-do-I-find-a-low-MOQ-clothing-manufacturer-for-my-startup-brand)*

```
Great question — and one of the most important decisions you'll make when starting a brand.

Here's what actually works in 2025:

**What "low MOQ" actually means:**
- 50–100 pieces per style/colour = genuinely low MOQ
- 200–300 pieces = standard market MOQ
- Anything below 50 = usually blank goods, not custom

**Where to find manufacturers with genuine low MOQ:**

1. **Google directly** — search "[garment type] manufacturer low MOQ OEM" — factories with websites often have better communication than those only on Alibaba
2. **Direct factory websites** — Alibaba is full of trading companies; look for factories with an actual facility address and audit certificates
3. **Trade shows** — MAGIC (Las Vegas) once a year is the best place to meet manufacturers face-to-face

**What to ask before committing:**
- Do you provide pre-production samples? (Always say yes to samples)
- Is printing/embroidery done in-house? (In-house = better QC)
- Do you provide AQL inspection reports? (This is your quality guarantee)
- What are your payment terms? (Industry standard: 30% deposit, 70% before shipment)

**Practical tip:** Start with a sample order only. Never approve bulk without a physical sample you've washed and measured.

We work with Potato Apparel (potatoapparel.com) — 50 pcs MOQ, Guangzhou factory, AQL 2.5 QC, samples in 7–10 days. Not the only option but a solid starting point if you're in early stages.
